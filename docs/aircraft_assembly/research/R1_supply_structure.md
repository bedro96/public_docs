# R1 – Boeing 737 (NG / MAX): Upstream Supply Chain & Structure Fabrication

## 1. Summary

- **Final assembly:** Every 737 is finally assembled at Boeing Renton, WA. A fourth line, the "North Line" at Everett, WA, started building 737 MAXs in July 2026. [verified] https://www.boeing.com/features/2026/07/everetts-new-north-line-begins-737-production
- **Fuselage:** The complete fuselage, with sections 41 through 48 joined, comes from Wichita, KS. It was built by Spirit AeroSystems, which Boeing acquired on 8 Dec 2025. It travels about 2,175 rail miles on a BNSF-routed dedicated flow. [verified] https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-12-08-Boeing-Completes-Acquisition-of-Spirit-AeroSystems
- **Wings:** The wings are not built by Spirit. Boeing Fabrication machines the skins and spars at Auburn and Frederickson, WA. They are assembled at Renton on automated panel and spar lines, in the first stage of the 737 flow. [verified-secondary] https://worldairlinenews.com/tag/boeing-fabrication/
- **Empennage:** Several of the hints in the brief about the tail are wrong or partial; see CORRECTIONS. KAI (Korea) builds MAX stabilizers; Tata Boeing (Hyderabad) builds vertical fin structures.
- **Part counts:** Boeing's own text gives 367,000 parts, about the same number of fasteners, and 36 miles (58 km) of wire. [verified-secondary]
- **Flow and rates:** Final assembly is a moving line at 2 in (5 cm) per minute, now nominally a "10-day flow". The rate history runs 31/mo (2005) → 42/mo (2014) → 52/mo (early 2019) → 38/mo (FAA cap Jan 2024) → 42/mo (Oct 2025) → 47/mo (May 2026).

## 2. Overall production flow and who makes what

### 2.1 Flow in one paragraph

Aluminium and composite parts are made across the US and the world. Spirit (Wichita) fabricates and joins the fuselage. Spirit also builds the pylons, nacelle parts and wing leading edges. Boeing Fabrication (Auburn/Frederickson) machines wing skins, stringers and spars. Boeing Renton builds the wings first, then assembles the aircraft on the moving line. The fuselage arrives by rail, wings and tail are mated, engines and the interior are installed, and the aircraft goes to the B1 flight and then to paint. [verified-secondary] https://simpleflying.com/putting-together-boeing-737-process-guide/

### 2.3 Cost split by system (Moody's 2019, via Wikipedia)

Aerostructures cost $10–12M, engines $7–9M, systems $5–6M, interiors $5–6M and avionics $1.5–2M per airplane. About 80% of 737 production cost goes to suppliers. [verified-secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX

Spirit supplies about 69% of the 737 airframe. [estimate/secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX

### 2.4 Spirit → Boeing re-integration

- Boeing completed the acquisition on 8 Dec 2025. It covers 737 fuselages and 767/777/787 structures, plus commercially procured P-8 and KC-46 fuselages. [verified] https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-12-08-Boeing-Completes-Acquisition-of-Spirit-AeroSystems
- About 15,000 employees across Wichita, Dallas, Tulsa and Prestwick joined Boeing. The Belfast remainder became "Short Brothers, a Boeing Company". Airbus took the A220 wing and mid-fuselage work, the Kinston and St Nazaire A350 fuselage plants, and Casablanca. [verified] same.

## 3. Rail delivery and rates

### 3.1 Train

- The Wichita→Renton route is about 2,175 miles (3,500 km) per Boeing text. [verified-secondary] https://web.archive.org/web/2023/http://www.b737.org.uk/production.htm
- Other sources give 1,700–1,800 miles, depending on the route counted. [estimate] https://simpleflying.com/putting-together-boeing-737-process-guide/
- Transit time is about 8 days in a 2013 account [https://www.gulfbusiness.com, not re-fetched]. A 2026 factory-tour report says "approximately two weeks" for the flow. [unverified, weak] https://aviationa2z.com/index.php/2026/10/13/how-boeing-builds-the-737-max-inside-the-world-of-aircraft-manufacturing/
- The user's hint of about 4 days is not supported.
- Fuselages are strapped on dedicated railcars, with the nose section attached to the centre and tail sections. [verified-secondary] https://web.archive.org/web/2023/http://www.b737.org.uk/production.htm
- BNSF operates the route, and the Renton plant has its own rail spur. [verified-secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renton_Boeing_Factory
- The number of railcars per train and fuselages per train: not found. [unverified]
- The old process held the fuselage about 13 days from arrival through final assembly. [verified-secondary] same b737.org.uk page.

### 3.2 2014 Montana derailment (useful narrative detail)

- On 3 July 2014 a train derailed near Superior/Alberton, Montana. 19 of 90 cars derailed, 6 fuselages were involved and 3 went into the Clark Fork River. All 6 were scrapped, roughly 14% of that month's capacity (42/mo). [verified-secondary, Reuters via Simple Flying and Seattle Times]

### 3.3 Fuselage rate

- Spirit shipped about 35 fuselages/month by 2013. The 10,000th 737 fuselage (line unit 6868) arrived Feb 2018. [verified-secondary]
- Spirit made 53/mo before the grounding, about 30/mo after Jan 2024, and has built 12,000+ fuselages in Wichita since the 1960s. [verified-secondary] NPR, 4 Jun 2024.

## 4. Fuselage fabrication at Spirit Wichita

### 4.1 Site

- Wichita is 650 acres with 8.1M sq ft of manufacturing space, 1.9M sq ft of offices and labs, and 2.8M sq ft of support/warehouse. Spirit as a whole has 20.8M sq ft across 11 facilities. Wichita–Tulsa is about 175 miles. [verified] Spirit FY2024 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364885/000162828025009088/0001628280-25-009088.txt

### 4.2 Capabilities

The 10-K lists these core competencies: "leading mechanized and automated assembly and fastening techniques", "large-scale skin fabrication", "chemical etching and metal bonding", and "precision metal forming". Chem-milling, stretch-forming and automated fastening are therefore confirmed only as generic corporate capabilities. Shop-level specifics for the 737 are not found. [verified (generic) / unverified (737-specific)] Same 10-K URL.

### 4.3 Sections

- Station sections run 41 (nose/cockpit), 43, 44 (wing area), 46, (47) and 48 (tail), per the Boeing 737-700 airport planning drawings and a PPRuNe discussion. The mid-exit door plug sits in Section 46. [verified-secondary] https://studylib.net/doc/25886772/techdata
- Boeing text says the fuselage is built in Wichita with the nose attached to the centre and tail. Which sections Spirit builds in house versus joins is [unverified].
- "Integration" is the station where sections are joined. NPR notes the Alaska 1282 door-plug rivet damage originated in Wichita, and "traveled work" is no longer allowed by Boeing. [verified-secondary] NPR, 4 Jun 2024.

### 4.4 Materials (NG, secondary)

- 2024 aluminium for fuselage skin, slats and flaps.
- 7075 for frames, stringers, keel/floor beams and wing ribs.
- 7178 for wing upper skin and spars.
- 7055 for wing lower skin.
- 7175 for the gear beam.
- Composites: graphite/epoxy rudder, elevators, ailerons, spoilers and thrust-reverser cowls; Kevlar fan cowls; fiberglass radome and tailcone.

[verified-secondary, NG era] https://web.archive.org/web/2023/http://www.b737.org.uk/production.htm

Rivet counts and per-fuselage shop hours were not found. [unverified]

### 4.5 Fastener count discrepancy

Boeing text says 367,000 parts and "an equal number of bolts, rivets and other fasteners". NPR says "more than 200,000 fasteners". They probably count differently (fuselage-only versus whole airplane, or different fastener classes). Use 367,000 for whole-airplane parts and treat 200,000+ as a different counting basis. [verified-secondary, conflict flagged]

Total parts including everything: about 600,000, "according to Boeing" for the 737NG. [verified-secondary]

## 5. Wing fabrication and build

1. **Machining (Auburn and Frederickson, WA).** Boeing Fabrication machines wing skins, stringers and spars. [verified-secondary] https://worldairlinenews.com/tag/boeing-fabrication/
2. **Assembly (Renton).** "Wings are the first 737 components to be assembled in the Renton production process." They use an automated panel assembly line (PAL) and automated spar assembly machines. The first MAX wings were built on the new "Central line". [verified-secondary] same.
3. **Automation.** An Electroimpact-built PAL sped up lower-skin assembly by 35%. The robotic spar line raised throughput by 33% (Wikipedia, citing Aviation Week). A 60-ton, 20-ft-tall PAL robot gives about 90% automation of wing assembly versus about 70% on legacy machines (ASM International video). [verified-secondary / estimate]
4. **Size.** The first MAX 7 wing spar was 65 ft long (Oct 2017, Wikipedia citing Flight). [verified-secondary]
5. **Wing-body join.** Wings are attached on days 4–5 of the final-assembly timeline in the sources below (they conflict). The detail "wing box mates inside section 46/centre section" from the brief is [unverified]. Boeing describes the wing as joined to the fuselage's centre section at the wing-body join station.
6. **Winglets.** The MAX AT winglet is dual-feather and gives up to 2% fuel saving. [verified] https://www.boeing.com/commercial/737max
7. **Everett.** Wings are trucked from Renton. [verified] North Line URL above.

Items not found: flap-track details, wing box dimensions beyond the spar length, and per-wing part counts. [unverified]

## 6. Engines, pylon, nacelle

LEAP-1B (Wikipedia, drawing on EASA data) [verified-secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International_LEAP:

- Fan diameter 69.4 in (1.76 m).
- Dry weight 2,780 kg.
- Length 3.147 m, width 2.421 m, height 2.256 m.
- Bypass ratio about 9:1.
- Takeoff thrust 130.41 kN; the MAX 8's LEAP-1B28 is 28,690 lbf.
- Fan, 3-stage LP compressor, 10-stage HP compressor, 2 HP and 5 LP turbine stages, TAPS II combustor.

The nacelle features chevrons for noise reduction. The nose-gear strut is 8 in taller to keep 17 in of ground clearance. The MAX 8 empty weight rose about 6,500 lb. [verified-secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX

Engines are installed late: "Day 9: engines mounted and connected" in the 10-day timeline, and "right before the airplane leaves final assembly" in Boeing text. [verified-secondary]

Not found: engine hang station numbers, number of attach bolts, and engine shipping details. [unverified]

## 7. Fasteners, sealant, paint, lean and the moving line

- **Moving line.** The line moves at 2 in (5 cm) per minute with timelines painted on the floor. Boeing says the 717 then the 737 were the first commercial airframe lines to move continuously. [verified-secondary] https://web.archive.org/web/2023/http://www.b737.org.uk/production.htm
- **Crane.** The overhead crane is 89 ft (27 m) above the floor. The tailfin is 23 ft (7 m) tall and is lifted early on the moving line. [verified-secondary] same.
- **Build time.** Reduced from 11 days (5,500 unit hours) toward 8 days (4,000 unit hours). The MAX is cited at about 10 days versus 11 for the NG. [verified-secondary] same and Simple Flying.
- **Pressure test.** The "high blow" test simulates 92,847 ft (28,300 m). Jacks lift the 154,983 lb (70,300 kg) airplane for the gear-retraction test. [verified-secondary] same.
- **Paint.** About 50 gal (189 L), roughly 300 lb (136 kg), applied after the B1 flight. It takes up to about 3 days. [verified-secondary/estimate] b737.org.uk and Simple Flying. Paint is done at Boeing Field. China deliveries are finished at Zhoushan. [verified-secondary]
- **Protective coating.** The fuselage gets a green protective coating on arrival. [unverified; one 2013 article]
- **Sealant:** no shop-level data found. [unverified]

### 7.2 Lean and flow

- Boeing's standard Renton flow is "10-day flow", and "long-flow" airplanes need more. [verified] https://www.boeing.com/features/2026/07/everetts-new-north-line-begins-737-production
- About 15 days from fuselage entering the line to the aircraft ready for inspection, with more than 2M parts fitted (counting all items). [unverified, weak] aviationa2z URL above.
- The long-run lean history (22→11→10 days) was not source-verified. [unverified]

## 11. CORRECTIONS to your hints

1. **MAX stabilizers.** The 737 MAX horizontal stabilizer is not from Frederickson. KAI (Korea) supplies the vertical and horizontal stabilizers (since 2022; contract extended to 2032). Frederickson machines wing skins and spars. Vertical fin structures are separately attributed to Tata Boeing (Hyderabad); the overlap with KAI is unresolved.
2. **Wing build location.** Wings are assembled at Renton from skins, stringers and spars machined at Auburn and Frederickson. They are not built at Auburn.
3. **Spirit and wings.** Spirit does not build the 737 wings. It builds the fuselage, pylons, nacelle structure/thrust reversers and wing leading edges.
4. **MAX wingspan.** It is 35.9 m, not 35.8 m. 35.8 m is the NG 737-800.
5. **MTOW.** Boeing's published MAX 8 figure is 82,640 kg (airport planning: 82,644 kg). 82,190/82,191 kg (181,200 lb) is the 737-8-200 / BBJ MAX 8 option, not the standard MAX 8.
6. **Rail transit time.** About 8 days is the best-supported number (one 2013 source), and one 2026 report says about two weeks. About 4 days is not supported. Distance is about 2,175 miles per Boeing text.
7. **FAA cap.** It went 38 → 42 (17 Oct 2025) → 47 (late May 2026). One source's claim that it was "lifted entirely in March 2026" is likely wrong.
8. **Confirmed.** 367,000 parts and 36 miles of wire are confirmed as Boeing text (for the NG). The LEAP-1B figures of 2,780 kg and 1.76 m are confirmed.
9. **Fastener count.** NPR's "more than 200,000 fasteners" conflicts with Boeing's "equal number" (~367,000). Likely different counting bases.
10. **Spirit.** It is now part of Boeing (closed 8 Dec 2025), so "Spirit" is no longer an independent supplier.
11. **Engine timing.** Engines are not on "positions 3–4". Every source places them late in the flow (day 9, or "right before the airplane leaves final assembly"). Older sources differ, as noted in the timeline.
12. **Rate history.** There is no single 31 → 38 → 42 sequence. The 38/mo is a 2024 FAA cap, not a 2000s step.
13. **Winglets.** The maker of the MAX split-tip winglets is unresolved: Kawasaki (NG-era list) versus GKN (Simple Flying).
14. **Everett.** The brief's "Boeing Everett? no – Renton" is half right. Renton is the main site, and Everett now has a fourth 737 line (North Line, July 2026).
