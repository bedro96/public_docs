# R2 — Boeing 737 (NG / MAX): Final Assembly in Renton, Testing and First Flight

**Purpose:** fact base for an animated isometric factory model whose finale is the finished plane taking off.
**Research date:** 2026-11-19. **Scope:** Part B only (Renton final assembly → test → first flight → delivery). Part A (sub-assemblies, Wichita fuselage build) is only touched where it affects Part B.

**Confidence tags** used on every number: **[verified]** = primary source (Boeing, FAA, TCDS, airport data) or two independent sources agree; **[verified-secondary]** = one reputable secondary source that is quoting/observing Boeing (tour reports, trade press); **[estimate]** = my calculation or an engineering approximation, method stated; **[unverified]** = only a forum post, an aggregator, or memory, or nothing found.

> Tool note: I could not read Boeing's time-lapse video transcripts (no transcript source reachable). Seattle Times and Aviation International News pages were paywalled/blocked, so I used the paragraphs that were visible plus the articles that quote them. The Boeing "737 production" article text was read via the archived copy on b737.org.uk, which reproduces Boeing's own text.

## 0. Executive summary

* Every 737 is final-assembled in the **Boeing Renton Factory** (Renton, WA), next to **Renton Municipal Airport (RNT, "Clayton Scott Field")**. The fuselage arrives **complete** (nose, center, tail sections already joined) by **BNSF rail from Wichita, Kansas**; the "section join" therefore happens in Wichita, *not* in Renton. Boeing closed its acquisition of Spirit AeroSystems (the Wichita fuselage maker) on 8 Dec 2025.
* Renton final assembly today runs on **three parallel moving lines (West, Central, East)**, not two. A **fourth 737 line ("North Line") in Everett** began building MAXs in 2026. "Line 1 / Line 2" is the older naming (Line 2 is the former 757 line).
* Takt/flow: Boeing's own press release (Jan 2005) says **11 days** (down from roughly 22 — "50 percent" reduction) at a line speed of **2 in/min (5 cm/min)**. A 2026 tour report describes a **10-"flow-day" sequence, ~15 calendar days** from fuselage rolling in to aircraft ready for inspection for the MAX.
* After the factory the airplane goes to the **Renton flight line** (fuel, engine runs, avionics, ~5 days per a 2013 report), makes its **first flight from Renton (RNT) and lands at Boeing Field (BFI/KBFI)**, where it is **painted, finished, customer-flown and delivered**. Aircraft at that point are still in **green corrosion-protection primer / protective film**.
* **Take-off finale facts that matter:** Renton's runway 16/34 is **only 5,382 ft (1,640 m)** — a MAX cannot do a max-weight take-off there, but production first flights are flown light. LEAP-1B28 take-off thrust is **130.41 kN (29,317 lbf) per engine** (TCDS, primary); engine weight ≤ **2,780 kg**. Rotation is **~2–3°/s toward ~15°** (Boeing FCTM as quoted by a study site).
* Production status (as of Nov 2026): FAA's **38/month cap (Jan 2024)** was raised to **42 (Oct 2025)** and, after an FAA "capstone review", to **47 (approved May 2026)**. Cumulative 737 deliveries are **~12,700**, not "11,000+".

### 1.2 Number of lines — "Line 1 / Line 2" is out of date

* **Three** final-assembly lines are operating in Renton: **West Line, Central Line, East Line**. The first three "flow days" take place on a raised level with fuselages stacked three abreast; on the night of flow-day 4 an overhead crane lowers each fuselage; about **one-third continue down the West Line, two-thirds are lifted through a purpose-built gap to the Central or East Line**. [verified-secondary] — https://www.pointhacks.com.au/news/boeing-737-renton-factory-tour-2026/ (Oct 2026 media tour). Corroborated by Flug Revue ("three 737 final-assembly lines in parallel in Renton") https://www.flugrevue.de/zivil/erste-boeing-737-10-in-everett-endmontiert/ and by AIN 2015 ("the Max line – the central line of the three final assembly lines in Renton") https://www.ainonline.com/aviation-news/aerospace/2015-06-12/ever-evolving-renton-factory-readies-boeing-737-max. [verified]
* Historical: the 757 (1,050 built 1981–2004) was built on "what is now line 2 of the 737 program" — AirlineReporter 2013. [verified-secondary] This is where "Line 1 / Line 2" comes from. In 2015 AIN already reported three lines.
* **Fourth line (North Line, Everett, building 40-26):** Boeing announced it Jan 2023; production started July 2026 at low rate; the first 737-10 completed there was rolled out in Oct 2026 (still green-primered). [verified-secondary] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX (text via API), https://www.aviationpros.com/aircraft-maintenance-technology/aircraft-technology/news/55380491/faa-approves-boeings-next-max-production-increase-ceo-ortberg-says (Seattle Times/TNS, May 2026: "fourth Max production line in Everett, known as the North Line … up and running this summer at a low rate"), https://www.flugrevue.de/zivil/erste-boeing-737-10-in-everett-endmontiert/.

### 1.3 The moving line

* **Speed:** "That line moves products from one assembly team to the next at the steady pace of **two inches per minute**" — Boeing press release, 27 Jan 2005. [verified] https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2005-01-27-Boeing-Reduces-737-Airplanes-Final-Assembly-Time-by-50-Percent
* **Stops only** for employee breaks, critical production issues or between shifts; **timelines painted on the floor** let workers gauge progress. [verified] (Boeing's production article, reproduced at https://web.archive.org/web/20240120014333/http://www.b737.org.uk/production.htm)
* **Conversion:** 2 in/min = 5.08 cm/min = 3.05 m/h ≈ **73 m per 24 h of uninterrupted motion** (one 39.5 m fuselage length ≈ 13 h). [estimate — arithmetic]. Because the line pauses (breaks, shift change), real daily travel is lower. Note this speed applies to the continuously moving segment, not the whole building.
* **History:** Lean techniques were introduced "in late 1999"; the 2005 release already describes the moving line as the "most compelling symbol" of lean. Boeing says it became "the first commercial airframe manufacturer to use the [moving line] concept to build jetliners when first the 717, and then the 737, production lines were transformed". [verified] (same Boeing text). The user's "introduced 2005–2006" is **wrong** — the line pre-dates January 2005. The exact start year for the 737 moving line is **[unverified]** (my recollection is 2001–2003; not confirmed by a source I could open). A supplier (ABW Technologies) states it has built moving-line platforms for Boeing since **2004**. [verified-secondary] https://www.abwtec.com/news/2018/4/12/featured-project-moving-line-platform-for-boeing-737
* **Tooling train:** moving-line platforms form "a long string of tools—ME, 2ME, 3ME, 4ME, and 5ME—which form a train that travels through the 737 plant as the aircraft moves from one station to the next. The train feeds the airplane with **power, air and hydraulics through umbilicals**." Platforms are aluminium with steel framing, fibreglass decking. [verified-secondary] https://www.abwtec.com/news/2018/4/12/featured-project-moving-line-platform-for-boeing-737
* **"Pulse" tooling:** AIN (June 2015) quotes Boeing's Renton leader: fuselage systems installation was consolidated "into a single, new **three-level moving design tool**… it will move in a **pulsing fashion**, much like our moving lines do, **so that the work moves to the mechanic**". AIN also reports a new automated **panel assembly line (PAL) that cuts flow time by 33 %**, and that the MAX line is the central of three lines with initial MAX build planned in three positions. [verified-secondary] https://www.ainonline.com/aviation-news/aerospace/2015-06-12/ever-evolving-renton-factory-readies-boeing-737-max (only the intro and a search-snippet of the paywalled text were readable). So the "Pulse line" is real but best described as a **pulsed (stop-and-go) systems-installation tool in the early positions**; the later positions use the continuous 2 in/min line.
* **AGVs / "autonomous guided vehicles":** I found **no source** showing AGVs carrying 737 fuselages/wings in Renton. **[unverified]**. Documented movers: overhead cranes, rubber-tired "dragsters" that roll the fuselage off the railcar (a reader comment on AirlineReporter's 2013 piece — [unverified]), and the moving-line platforms above. Laser alignment is documented for wing placement (Simple Flying: wings "craned into place and aligned via lasers" [verified-secondary]). Recommendation: animate cranes and tool-trains, not AGVs, unless you label it artistic license.

## 2. Station-by-station sequence (animation script)

### 2.0 Upstream context (arrives at the factory)

* **Fuselage sections:** a 737 fuselage is built as four sections — **41 (nose/forward fuselage incl. radome area), 43 (forward body), 46 (aft body), 48 (tail cone/aft, APU & horizontal-stabilizer attach)**. The 41/43/46/48 naming is verified for the 737 family (Boeing airport-planning body-station drawing shows "Section 41" and "Section 43"; Chinese-language aviation English text quoting the 737 maintenance material says "divided into four sections: 41, 43, 46, 48"). [verified-secondary] https://studylib.net/doc/25886772/techdata, https://wenku.baidu.com/view/9298ce1a59eef8c75ebfb307.html. Boeing's own text: Wichita employees "attach the nose section … to the center and tail sections" in an **integration tool**; the result is a **single-piece fuselage** shipped complete. [verified] (Boeing article at b737.org.uk; Boeing Images caption https://secure.boeingimages.com/archive/737-900-Fuselage-Assembly-at-Wichita-Factory-2F3XC57ZM4Q.html). **Correction:** the join is done in Wichita, not Renton.
* **Rail:** **2,175 mi (3,500 km)**; Boeing text (archive link). In 1997 Boeing quoted 2,190 miles for the first 737-800 fuselage; transit time ~8 days (2013) to ~2 weeks (2026 tour). [verified / verified-secondary]
* **Ownership:** Spirit AeroSystems → Boeing, closed 8 Dec 2025 (~15,000 staff across 5 sites). [verified] https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-12-08-Boeing-Completes-Acquisition-of-Spirit-AeroSystems
* **Green colour:** the green is **corrosion-protection primer plus a strippable protective film/coating**, not "paint". Sources disagree on chemistry: one says zinc-chromate-type yellow tinted green with lamp black; another says primer + peelable Temporary Protective Coating ("Spraylat"). The brand name "Koropon" that you suggested is **not confirmed** in the sources I could open. [verified-secondary for "primer + film"; **[unverified]** for "Koropon"] https://simpleflying.com/why-are-new-boeing-fuselages-green/, https://boltflight.com/green-fuselages-chromate-free-primers-the-real-reason-boeing-and-airbus-look-different-before-delivery/

**Which airport? (precise answer):** Boeing 737s **take off from Renton Municipal Airport (RNT/KRNT), 16/34**, immediately beside the factory, and **land at Boeing Field (BFI/KBFI)**, ~5 mi north-north-west, where painting, final fit-out, the customer flight and delivery take place. Evidence: 737-700 first flight 9 Feb 1997 took off from Renton and landed at Boeing Field (https://boeing.mediaroom.com/1997-02-09-Boeing-Next-Generation-737-Takes-to-the-Skies); MAX 8 first flight 29 Jan 2016, **Runway 34 at Renton**, flew 2 h 47 min, landed at Boeing Field 12:35 pm (http://www.b737.org.uk/maxflttestprog.htm); MAX 9 (13 Apr 2017, 2 h 42 min), MAX 7 (16 Mar 2018), MAX 10 (18 Jun 2021 via touch-and-go at Moses Lake) all Renton → Boeing Field. [verified / verified-secondary]. Planes are **not** flown from BFI in their "first flight".

**Unresolved ordering detail:** Boeing's older text says paint comes *before* the Boeing test flight ("towed to a hangar for painting … ready for a Boeing test flight"), while 2011–2016 tour sources say Renton first flight → BFI → paint. The simplest consistent storyline for the animation (my synthesis): **Renton flight line → first flight RNT→BFI (green) → BFI paint hangar → B-1/C-1 test and customer flights from BFI → delivery.** Whether the initial RNT departure is itself called "B-1" (as the 2013 report implies) is **[unverified]**.

**About the "8.6 psi" number — correction:** The 737 NG/MAX pressurisation system is limited by **relief valves at 9.10 psi** (737-600/700/800/900 grouping) — 8.65 psi on 737-100/500 — and the controller normally holds **8.35 psi above FL370**, giving a **cabin altitude of ~8,000 ft at the 41,000 ft ceiling**.
